# Triton/vLLM DoW rerun in three machine windows

Campaign `extension_dow_rescoped`. It replaces the Triton and vLLM-standalone
arms of the closed campaign `extension_dow_screening`. Everything named here
is generated by `scripts/generate_rerun_windows.py`. Never hand-edit the
descriptors or the cells; regenerate them and check with `--check`.

## 1. Why a rerun, and why in windows

On 2026-11-06 the original screening was found to monitor Triton and vLLM
standalone with an incomplete process scope. Triton sampled only the backend
and engine. vLLM sampled only the API server, without the EngineCore. Those
runs cannot be compared with the Dynamo arm, whose scope (every component
PGID) is correct and complete (19/19 runs). The 38 Triton/vLLM runs (16 design
points plus 3 center points per system, 36 h each) are therefore repeated with
the whole-container v2 scope (`docs/container_scope.md`).

Run serially, they take about 57 days of machine time. The host cannot be held
that long, so the rerun is split into three windows of at most 25 days. Each
window has its own state file. Each window is also calibrated just before it
starts: method v2.1, a fresh stack per sweep, calibrations older than 30 days
refused. Two windows therefore never share a calibration, and a calibration
cannot age across the gap between windows.

The cells are the existing generated container_tree v2 cells
(`cells/dow/dow_triton_*`, `cells/dow/dow_vllm_*`). Keeping their ids lets
the analysis join the same design matrix. The new `campaign_id` keeps the run
directories (`extension_dow_rescoped_<cell>_r01`) separate from the closed
campaign's directories.

## 2. Design: quarter blocks

The design is the 2^(5-1) Resolution V fraction, I = ABCDE (E = ABCD). For each
system, the signs of the AC and AD contrasts split the 16 points into four
quarter blocks of 4 points each. The generalized interaction of AC and AD is CD.

Inside a quarter block, AC and AD are constant. As a result, any quarter block,
and any union of quarter blocks, is orthogonal to:

- all five main effects;
- the seven two-factor interactions other than AC, AD and CD.

Only AC, AD and CD can be correlated with window membership. In the design
they are aliased with BDE, BCE and ABE respectively. This is asserted by the
generator and by `tests/test_rerun_windows.py`.

| System | W1 | W2 | W3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triton | AC+AD+ and AC+AD- (8 points) + cp1 | AC-AD+ (4) + cp2 | AC-AD- (4) + cp3 |
| vLLM | AC+AD- (4) + cp1 | AC+AD+ and AC-AD+ (8) + cp2 | AC-AD- (4) + cp3 |
| Dynamo | (none) | (none) | cp4 (bridge, 48 h) |
| Runs | 14 | 14 | 11 |

The two systems use different unions on purpose. If the CP correction left a
window shift uncorrected, it would bias AC in Triton (W1 against the other
windows) and AD in vLLM (W2 against the others), not the same contrast in both
systems.

**Order inside a window.** The seed is fixed (20261120). Within each system the
design points are shuffled. The minority system is spread through the majority
as evenly as the 8/4 split allows, so no system ever has more than two
consecutive runs. Each system's center point sits in the middle of the window,
and in W3 `dow_dynamo_disagg_cp4` sits at the exact middle. The order is listed
in each descriptor header and in `rerun_design_matrix.csv`.

**Bridge cell `dow_dynamo_disagg_cp4`.** It uses the same template, workload
and 48 h window as cp1 to cp3. Its only difference is that it turns on the
per-process split (`monitors.components.per_process_split: true`): every
prefill/decode worker PGID is then broken down into `dynamo_main`,
`vllm_enginecore` and `other`. It measures the shift between the completed
Dynamo epoch and the rerun epoch at the design center. It also resolves
whether the Dynamo growth sits in the orchestration code or in the inference
engine inside the workers. The default for every other Dynamo cell stays off,
so the 19 completed runs keep their exact sampler and schema.

**Projected durations** (asserted; the generator refuses a plan that breaks
them). The calibration figure is 17 h per 14 cells, the measured v2 pace. The
per-run overhead is 0.5 h (bring-up, client drain, teardown, VRAM quiescence,
10-minute inter-run cooldown). Setup is 2 h per window (reboot, snapshot,
dry-runs). The retry margin must be at least max(1.5 days, the longest run in
the window).

| Window | Runs | Soak | Calibration | Runs + overhead + setup | Total | Retry margin in 25 d | Calibration age at last dispatch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | 504 h | 12.5 h | 17.0 h | 8 h + 2 h | 22.6 d | 2.4 d | ~19.8 d |
| W2 | 504 h | none | 17.0 h | 7 h + 2 h | 22.1 d | 2.9 d | ~19.8 d |
| W3 | 408 h | none | 13.4 h | 5.5 h + 2 h | 17.9 d | 7.1 d | ~15.7 d |

## 3. The CP-based window correction: assumption, stated explicitly

`analysis/window_effects.py` estimates one offset per window from the center
points and subtracts it from every run of that window. The method rests on the
following assumption:

> **Assumption W.** The window effect is additive on the analysis scale
> (additive with `--scale linear`, multiplicative with `--scale log`). It is
> the same for every design point of a system within a window, so there is no
> window x factor interaction. The center point of that window measures it.
> The default `--correction common` additionally assumes that the shift is the
> same for Triton and vLLM.

What the design guarantees, and what it does not:

- **What the blocking protects against.** Each window holds a balanced
  subset of each system's runs, so the five main effects and the seven
  non-window interactions are orthogonal to the window indicators. That
  protects them from a window offset that is **additive on the analysis
  scale**: one constant per window (per system), the same at every design
  point. Only AC, AD and CD are exposed to such an offset. This holds with or
  without the correction.
- **What it does not protect against.** A window x factor interaction, i.e. a
  shift whose size depends on a factor level, is not an additive offset. It
  biases the corresponding contrasts, main effects included: a window x A
  interaction produces an apparent A main effect. A shift that is
  multiplicative while the analysis runs on `--scale linear` (or additive
  under `--scale log`) is such an interaction on the analysis scale.
  `tests/test_window_effects.py` (StatedLimit) shows the A bias surviving
  the correction.
- **With the correction**, AC, AD and CD are recovered under Assumption W. The
  correction leaves the other twelve effects unchanged, and the report prints
  the largest such change as a check (it must be about 0).
- **The correction cannot detect or remove a window x factor interaction.**
  The center points sit at the design center, so they measure the shift at
  that point only, and the design has no replicate points within a window to
  test the interaction. Treat Assumption W as a stated assumption in the
  paper's validity threats, for the main effects as much as for AC, AD and CD.
- **The correction carries CP noise.** Each corrected window effect inherits
  the error of one CP difference (two CP runs). Report AC, AD and CD both with
  and without the correction.
- **Consistency check.** Triton and vLLM measure the same window shift
  independently. The check needs all six center points (two systems x three
  windows). With one missing it reports INCOMPLETE, never CONSISTENT, and the
  common correction is disabled. If their offsets differ by more than 10% of
  the CP level (`--consistency-tol`), the common assumption fails and the
  common correction is also disabled. In that case use
  `--correction per_system`, which uses each system's own CP, and say so in
  the paper.
- **The bridge (cp4 against cp1..cp3)** measures the Dynamo epoch shift at the
  design center only, expressed as an absolute value, as a percentage and in
  cp1..cp3 standard deviations. It qualifies cross-system comparisons between
  the Dynamo arm and the rerun epoch. It is not used to correct either arm.

Response per run (default `growth_kb_per_req`): the Theil-Sen slope of the
post-warmup engine USS (5-minute medians), divided by the served request rate,
with 1 KB = 1000 bytes. On the 19 completed Dynamo runs the script reproduces
the 2026-11-20 first look: B +9.06, C +1.25, E +1.38, A -0.41, D -0.14 KB/req,
and no interaction above Lenth's margin. The first look used 1024-byte KB, so
its values are 2.4% lower.

## 4. Supporting checks

- **Closed original campaign (R1).** `campaign.py --close` marks
  `state/dow_campaign_state.json` CLOSED. From then on, `--start`, `--resume`
  and `--dry-run` all exit 11, and no ALERT or status text suggests a resume.
  The original state is preserved byte-identical under `state/closed/`.
  `--close`, `--start` and `--resume` share an exclusive lock on
  `<state>.lock`, held by a live orchestrator for its whole life: a close
  cannot land between a start's closure check and its state delete, and a
  running campaign cannot be closed (both are refused with exit 3).
- **Burstiness (R7).** The validator compares the realized interarrival CoV
  with the theoretical CoV of the client's on/off process, using the recorded
  rate, burst_factor and burst_on_seconds. The band is
  max(4 SE, 3% of theory). For `arrival_mode: constant` (theoretical CoV 0) it
  is a dispatch-jitter allowance of sqrt(2) x 50 ms x rate; see
  `analysis/arrival_theory.py`. All 19 Dynamo runs
  pass, including p11 (1.460 against 1.440). Analyze factor E with the realized
  CoV alongside the coded level: at low absolute rates the same nominal level
  gives weaker bursts.
- **PSS (R6).** `aging_trends.py` and the validator report the PSS trend next
  to USS for every aggregate and component. When PSS - USS grows significantly
  and materially, they issue a WARNING (never a failure): that pattern means
  shared memory is growing where USS cannot see it. On the completed Dynamo
  runs the gap is constant, about 300 MB per worker.

## 5. Operator sequence

Run everything on the server from `~/wosar/llm-serving-bench` in the `wosar`
conda environment, after `git pull --ff-only`. Each step lists its acceptance
criteria. **A failed criterion stops the sequence**: fix the problem, then
repeat the step.

### Step 0, once before W1: close the original campaign

```bash
python3 scripts/campaign.py --campaign-yaml campaigns/extension/dow_campaign.yaml \
  --close --reason "superseded: incomplete Triton/vLLM process scope, rerun under extension_dow_rescoped"
python3 scripts/campaign.py --campaign-yaml campaigns/extension/dow_campaign.yaml --dry-run; echo "rc=$?"
```

Acceptance:

- the close exits 0;
- the dry-run prints `REFUSED ... CLOSED` and `rc=11`;
- `state/closed/dow_campaign_state_<stamp>/` holds the byte copy and `CLOSED.json`
  (`state_sha256_before_close`);
- `bash scripts/longtest_status.sh` shows `CLOSED` and no `--resume`;
- the 44 completed runs, the interrupted vLLM p05 run directory and
  `state/calibration/` are untouched.

### Per window k = 1, 2, 3

**1. Reboot.** A planned, orderly reboot (`sudo systemctl reboot`), so every
window starts from a fresh host.

Acceptance:

- `last -x` shows an orderly shutdown and boot;
- `nvidia-smi` shows 4 GPUs, no compute processes, and driver 580.159.03;
- `docker images --digests` matches `engines/*/image_pin_ext.json`;
- the nightly timers are still masked;
- `/etc/cron.d/wdt` (IPMI watchdog) is present and untouched;
- NTP is synchronized.

**2. Environment snapshot.**

```bash
snapshot="$HOME/wosar/ops/$(date -u +%Y%m%dT%H%M%SZ)_rerun_w<k>"
mkdir -p "$snapshot"
cp -a campaigns/extension/state "$snapshot/state"
git rev-parse HEAD > "$snapshot/git_sha.txt"
uname -a > "$snapshot/kernel.txt"
nvidia-smi > "$snapshot/nvidia-smi.txt"
docker images --digests > "$snapshot/images.txt"
systemctl list-timers --all > "$snapshot/timers.txt"
python3 scripts/generate_rerun_windows.py --check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests
```

Acceptance:

- all files are written;
- `--check` prints `Rerun tree is up to date.`;
- the full test suite passes;
- the kernel and driver match the previous window, or the change is recorded
  as an environment block in EXPERIMENT_STATE.md before going on;
- the VRAM baseline is recorded. The 0 MiB versus 610 MiB discrepancy seen on
  2026-11-17 must be explained before W1.

**3. Soak, W1 only, before calibration.**

```bash
tmux new -d -s rerun_soak \
  'python3 scripts/campaign.py --campaign-yaml campaigns/extension/dow_rerun_w1_soak.yaml --start'
# after both runs completed (about 13.5 h):
for s in triton vllm; do
  python3 analysis/soak_check.py --run-dir "$HOME/wosar/runs/extension_dow_rescoped_soak_${s}_r01" \
    --json "$HOME/wosar/ops/soak_${s}.json"
done
```

Acceptance (`SOAK: PASS` for both systems):

- `validate_extension_run.py --scope-only` passes;
- the run actually lasted at least 6 h: the effective duration is the shorter of
  the recorded wall time and the sampled span of the scope aggregate, with no
  sampling gap over three periods (the declared `duration_s` does not count);
- there are zero identity-loss or latched events;
- every tick has complete scope (100%);
- the model-holding GPU process is inside the engine subtree on every tick;
- no process identity disappeared;
- every process-set addition listed from `scope_membership.jsonl` has been read
  and explained (for example a late helper process).

Any FAIL means the 36 h runs would abort on the same event: do not calibrate.

**4. Calibration of window k.**

```bash
python3 scripts/calibrate_dow.py --campaign-yaml campaigns/extension/dow_rerun_w<k>.yaml --dry-run
tmux new -d -s calib_w<k> \
  'python3 scripts/calibrate_dow.py --campaign-yaml campaigns/extension/dow_rerun_w<k>.yaml'
```

Acceptance:

- the dry-run lists exactly the window's cells, all writing to
  `state/calibration_rerun/w<k>/`, with a projected duration of 17.0 h (W1, W2)
  or 13.4 h (W3);
- the real run ends with `SUMMARY: N cells -- N ok`;
- the run ends with `VERDICT: dry-run clean -- 0 MISSING`;
- there are no engine_failure cells, or each one was re-run with
  `--recalibrate <cell>` until ok;
- the two systems' CP ceilings in the window are mutually consistent. The
  2026-07-20 finding is that co-tenant nightly jobs can halve a ceiling, so
  re-take a divergent CP.

calibrate_dow refuses a CLOSED descriptor (exit 11) and the soak descriptor
(exit 3).

```bash

bash scripts/campaign_health.sh --campaign-yaml campaigns/extension/dow_rerun_w<k>.yaml
python3 analysis/validate_extension_run.py --run-dir <run_dir>    # each completed run
```

Acceptance:

- there is no ALERT;
- health exits 0 (1 = inspect when convenient, 2 = act now);
- every completed run passes the full gate (`GATE: PASS`): integrity, container
  scope, client features with the CoV check, analysis;
- any PSS - USS WARNING is noted in EXPERIMENT_STATE.md.

In W3, check `perproc_identities.jsonl` of `dow_dynamo_disagg_cp4` within its
first minutes: each of `dynamo_prefill` and `dynamo_decode` must show one
`dynamo_main` and at least one `vllm_enginecore`. The validator's `[2c]` check
(per-process sums equal the component sums) must pass. The tag relies on
vLLM's process title. This is its first hardware observation, so record what
you see.

**8. Close-out of the window.**

Acceptance:

- the queue exits 0, with all runs completed and no failed runs;
- the runs are tarred on the server at nice 19 and copied to the Mac;
- the off-box validation passes;
- EXPERIMENT_STATE.md records the window: dates, kernel and driver, any retries.

The next window may start after any gap. Its calibration is new by
construction.
